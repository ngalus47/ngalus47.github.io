About Me

Introduction

I am a computer engineering student at St. Mary's University in San Antonio with a passion for bridging the gap between hardware and software through embedded systems. My interests are rooted in building, designing, and experimenting with systems that bring ideas to life, from vehicles and robotics to electronics and software. I view myself as both a problem-solver and a creator, driven to explore how engineering can make technology more efficient, more reliable, and more accessible.

Early Life

I was born into a culturally diverse family with Hispanic, Filipino, and Ukrainian roots, which shaped my ability to see challenges from multiple perspectives and taught me the value of adaptability and resilience. From an early age, I was drawn toward hands-on problem-solving.

Much of my foundation came from working with my parents. With my mom, I was immersed in LEGO robotics league competitions, where I learned how programming and electronics could be used to create moving, thinking systems. I not only watched but also helped, sparking my curiosity in circuits, coding, and how machines functioned. With my dad, I dove into the mechanical side of engineering. I helped him on projects like rebuilding a truck engine with a cam and long-tube headers, installing a six-inch lift, and tackling everyday fixes that required patience, precision, and creativity. These experiences didn't just give me skills with tools — they instilled confidence that I could understand, improve, and eventually design complex systems myself.

Academics

At NSITE – Holmes High School, I grew academically and as a leader. I was honored as a Service Academy Congressional Nominee, named a UIL Football Academic All-State honoree, and placed second in district for UIL Computer Science. Leadership defined much of my high school experience as well: I was Varsity Football Defensive Captain, NHS Chapter Secretary, and UIL Computer Science Club Captain, where I motivated peers, led by example, and balanced academics with extracurriculars. These years taught me how to manage responsibilities while keeping sight of long-term goals.

At St. Mary's University, I've carried that momentum forward. I am pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Computer Engineering with a current GPA of 3.81, supported by scholarships including the Honors Scholar Award, Trustees' Scholarship, Jack C. Jordan Scholarship, William Ludwikowski Scholarship, and Valero Alamo Bowl Scholarship. My coursework has included C Programming, Java, Circuits Analysis, Logic Design, Physics, Calculus, Discrete Math, and Advanced Math for Engineers, all of which directly connect to the systems I love to build.

Leadership and service remain central to who I am. I serve as Treasurer of IEEE, where I manage budgets, plan projects, and help shape the direction of the chapter. As a President's Ambassador, I represent St. Mary's to prospective students and external partners. Within the Honors Program, I sit on the Honors Scholar Leadership Council, contributing to academic and social programming for my peers. I also serve as a Resident Assistant (RA), where I support and mentor other students, and as Project Manager for the UAV Club, where I guide technical projects that combine design, programming, and teamwork. These roles give me a chance to practice leadership not just in theory, but in real-world, high-responsibility contexts.

Personal Interests & Hobbies

Outside of school, I invest my time in activities that let me combine creativity with engineering and discipline.

In automotive engineering, I work on everything from routine maintenance to performance upgrades, including engine and transmission rebuilding, installing aftermarket components, and fine-tuning vehicles for reliability and speed. My interest extends into the electrical side as well, where I build custom wiring harnesses for lighting systems, horns, and interior/exterior controls. I have also begun applying 3D printing to design and fabricate engine tools and switch housings, blending mechanical and digital design.

In technology and robotics, I work on electronics, circuit design, and programming to create integrated systems. I enjoy working on remote-controlled cars, modifying them to maximize speed, durability, and performance, which gives me small-scale testbeds for real engineering challenges. I also use 3D modeling and 3D printing to develop creative solutions like a mechanical protein dispenser and ergonomic tools to make everyday life easier.

Fitness and martial arts also play a role in shaping me. I am a 2nd Degree Black Belt in Tae Kwon Do and have worked as a junior instructor, experiences that taught me discipline, patience, and teaching skills. In addition, I train in powerlifting, swimming, biking, and endurance activities, which give me balance, persistence, and focus — qualities I bring into engineering challenges as well.

Side Projects & Engineering Work

What excites me most is taking knowledge from the classroom and applying it to projects that challenge me to design, integrate, and innovate.

One of my early builds was a light control module, an embedded system project where I integrated hardware and software to regulate outputs efficiently. I am now working on a mock engine controller, using LEDs and servos to simulate how an engine control module regulates different processes. Projects like this not only give me hands-on experience but also prepare me for the kind of systems work done at organizations like Holt, International, and Southwest Research Institute.

Beyond embedded systems, I push my skills with 3D modeling and fabrication, creating both automotive-related tools and personal-use devices like switch housings and everyday problem-solving gadgets. These side projects give me a chance to be inventive, bridging mechanical, electrical, and software domains to build systems that are practical, reliable, and efficient.
