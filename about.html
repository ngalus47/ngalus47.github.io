About Me

Introduction

I am a computer engineering student at St. Mary's University in San Antonio (Honors Program, BS/MS track) with a passion for bridging the gap between hardware and software through embedded systems. My interests are rooted in building, designing, and experimenting with systems that bring ideas to life, from vehicles and robotics to electronics and software. I view myself as both a problem-solver and a creator, driven to explore how engineering can make technology more efficient, more reliable, and more accessible. My goal is a career in engine controls for automotive and powersport.

Early Life

I was born into a culturally diverse family with Hispanic, Filipino, and Ukrainian roots, which shaped my ability to see challenges from multiple perspectives and taught me the value of adaptability and resilience. From an early age, I was drawn toward hands-on problem-solving.

Much of my foundation came from working with my parents. With my mom, I was immersed in LEGO robotics league competitions, where I learned how programming and electronics could be used to create moving, thinking systems. I not only watched but also helped, sparking my curiosity in circuits, coding, and how machines functioned. With my dad, I dove into the mechanical side of engineering. I helped him on projects like rebuilding a truck engine with a cam and long-tube headers, installing a six-inch lift, and tackling everyday fixes that required patience, precision, and creativity. These experiences didn't just give me skills with tools. They instilled confidence that I could understand, improve, and eventually design complex systems myself.

Projects & Engineering Work

What excites me most is taking knowledge from the classroom and applying it to projects that challenge me to design, integrate, and innovate. Here are the ones I am proudest of.

BEMC "Copper Fangs" PCB Robot

I led a team of 4 in the Bright Electronics Manufacturing Challenge, a national PCB design and build competition. We designed a robot from the board up in Altium Designer, built around a PIC18F26Q43 microcontroller with fuzzy logic control across 7 IR sensors, plus a custom circuit that boosts a 5V input to a 7.1V output. I contributed to every part, from PCB design to code to assembly, and worked alongside three teammates, an EE hardware designer, an EE on assembly and mechanical, and a CE programmer. We placed 5th nationally in the design round, as 1 of 10 national finalists from 31 teams, then made it to the quarterfinals of the race.

The part I remember most is the pressure. A component shorted during testing the night before we flew out, so I made a plan with backups: I called the hotel to confirm it could receive a package, overnighted a replacement from DigiKey, and when shipping slipped, the team bought equivalent parts at Micro Center. The DigiKey package showed up in time, but we raced with a backup plan ready, and I learned a lot about staying calm and planning for failure.

Rust vs C Drive-by-Wire Throttle Thesis

My honors thesis, planned to start this winter with Dr. Michael Yuhas as my advisor, compares Rust and C for a safety-critical drive-by-wire throttle control loop. I will build a bench test rig with fault injection, collect data through the spring, and write it up for publication next fall. Throttle control is a core engine controls function, so this project ties directly to where I want my career to go.

Permafrost Thaw Research

In Spring 2025 I worked with a classmate and faculty advisor Dr. Kaitlin Hill to model permafrost thaw by coupling the Budyko energy balance model with a 1D heat transfer equation. I built and ran the Budyko model in Python, and we simulated the system with Euler and finite difference methods. We presented at the 2025 Texas Undergraduate Mathematics Conference and the St. Mary's Spring Research Showcase.
